# Social comments — Teacher guide

The Social comments block lets participants talk about a page of your course right where it is:
ask a question about a reading, share a tip for an assignment, organise a study group or give each
other feedback. Comments are short, replies are one level deep, and everyone can pin what they want
to find again and subscribe to the pages they follow.

## Adding the block

1. Turn **Edit mode** on.
2. Open the page where people should talk: the course page or an activity page.
3. Choose **Add a block** and pick **Social Comments**.

Comments belong to the page they are written on, not to the block. A block on the course page
collects comments about the course; a block on an activity page collects comments about that
activity. Add the block to every page where you want a conversation.

Removing the block hides the conversation, but does not end it: the comments stay in the comments
report and on the pinboards. To stop comments on a page, prevent **Post comments**
(`block/socialcomments:postcomments`) in the permissions of the activity or the course. To remove
comments, delete them in the report.

To show the block on every page of the course, add it to the course page, configure it and set
**Display on page types** to *Any page*. Every page then has its own comments.

On a category page, the block shows no comments. Configured for *Any page* in the category, it
appears on the pages of all its courses.

## Ideas for using it

- **A course café** on the course page: introductions, finding study partners, sharing useful
  links.
- **Questions about a reading** on a page or a book: students explain things to each other, you
  confirm or correct.
- **An FAQ for an assignment**: answer a question once for everybody. Students pin the answers.
- **Team coordination** in activities with separate groups: each group plans its work in its own
  comments.
- **Peer feedback**: students ask each other for feedback before submitting.
- **Revision**: students pin a whole page and find all its comments on their pinboard before the
  exam.

## Comments and replies

Write into the text field at the top of the block and choose **Post**. The comment appears at the
end of the list; people who are subscribed to the page get it in their next digest. Writing a
comment subscribes you to the page.

**Reply** below a comment opens a reply field. The menu (⋮) of a comment or reply offers **Edit**
(your own posts) and **Delete** (your own, or all posts with the capability, which teachers have).
Deleting a comment deletes its replies too.

Long conversations are split into pages; the newest comment is on the last page. Of many replies,
only the newest are shown; **Show earlier replies** shows the others.

## Groups

In activities with **separate groups**, students see the comments of their own groups and the
comments for all groups. With more than one group to choose from, a menu next to **Post** decides
who sees the comment; teachers, who see all groups, can also post to *All groups*. In separate
groups, each comment shows its group. In visible groups and without groups, everybody sees all
comments.

## Pins and the pinboard

The pin of a comment adds it to your **pinboard**; the pin at the bottom of the block pins all
comments of the page, including future ones. **Pinboard** at the bottom of the block opens the
pinboard of the course, with a menu to switch to your other courses. Pins are personal: nobody else
sees what you pinned.

To remove the pins from the block, configure it and set **Hide pins** to *Yes*.

## Subscriptions and digests

The **Subscribe** switch in the block subscribes to the comments of the page. Subscribers get a
digest of the new comments and replies of others, as a Moodle notification (and by email,
depending on their notification preferences). Your own posts are not in your digest.

## The comments report

**Comments report** at the bottom of the block (and in the course's *Reports*) has two tabs:

- **New comments**: what was posted since your last login, by page.
- **All comments**: a table of all comments you can see, with filters for section, activity, date,
  author and text, and a link to delete each comment.

Both show only the pages and groups you can see.

## Dates

Depending on the site setting, posts show how long ago they were written ("5 minutes ago",
"Yesterday", "3 weeks ago") or the date and time. Pointing at the date shows the exact date and
time.

## Real-time updates

If your site has set up the updating service of the Deft response block, new, edited and deleted
comments appear for everybody who has the page open, without reloading it. Otherwise they appear
when the page is opened again or another page of comments is loaded.
